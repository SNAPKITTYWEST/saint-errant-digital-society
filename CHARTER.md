## Article I: Name and Nature

The organization is named **Saint Errant Digital Society** (the "Society").

The Society is a non-governmental, non-profit digital collective. It is not incorporated. It operates as an open community of practice under the stewardship of its founders.

The Society is affiliated with the SnapKitty ecosystem but is independently operated as a public educational and community resource.

## Article II: Purpose

The Society exists to:

1. **Build sovereign infrastructure** — tools, libraries, and systems that communities own and control
2. **Preserve craft knowledge** — guides, tutorials, and documentation that teach the WHY, not just the HOW
3. **Recognize demonstrated excellence** — through the Purple Hat system, not credential or credential
4. **Connect errant builders** — a community of independent craftspeople working together without a common employer

## Article III: Membership

Membership is open to any person who completes the Member Introduction process.

There is no fee. There is no exclusion by background, geography, gender, education, or experience level.

The only standard is the work.

## Article IV: The Purple Hat

The Purple Hat is the Society's emblem of recognized craft. It is earned through the Trials system and conferred by peer review from two existing members.

The Purple Hat is permanent once earned. It cannot be revoked except for material violation of the Code (Article VI).

## Article V: Governance

The Society is governed by its founding members:

- **Jessica Westerhoff** `@SNAPKITTYWEST` — Sovereign Proprietor
- **Ahmad Ali Parr** `@AHMADALIPARR` — Chief Architect

Decisions that affect the Society's direction, charter, or affiliation require agreement of both founding members.

Day-to-day operations — trial reviews, project approvals, guide merges — are carried by any Adept-grade or above member.

## Article VI: The Code

The following are grounds for membership review and potential removal:

1. Using Society resources or affiliation to cause harm to individuals
2. Misrepresenting your grade or earned credentials
3. Submitting work that is not your own without attribution
4. Using the Society's name to deceive third parties

Nothing else is grounds for removal. Disagreement, different opinions, failure at a trial, or inactivity are never grounds for removal.

## Article VII: The Commons

All work produced under the Society's banner and submitted to this repository is MIT licensed and belongs to the Commons.

Contributors retain credit and attribution. The Society retains the right to publish, modify, and distribute all submitted work under MIT terms.

## Article VIII: Amendment

This charter may be amended by agreement of the founding members, with 30-day notice to all members via the Discord server.
